description: "Why liquidity here cannot be pulled, and the one honest consequence of that."

A new pool starts with 99% of the supply on one side and nothing on the
other - the other side fills up as people buy.

The sell floor is a property of single-sided v4 liquidity, and holds on Base and Robinhood Chain alike.

That one design choice is behind the two biggest guarantees on the venue:

- **Nobody can pull the liquidity** - the launcher holds nothing after
  launch and no admin exists over the pool.
- **The launch price is a floor** - the pool cannot pay out below the
  price it opened at, because below that price there is nothing on the
  other side to pay with.

## The one honest consequence

While a token still trades near its launch price, the pool has not
collected much of the pair asset yet. A very large sell can ask for more
than the pool holds - and the whole transaction is **refused** rather
than partially filled.

<Note>
  This is not a honeypot and nothing is locked: selling in smaller pieces
  always works, and the interface tells you when a sell is too big
  instead of letting it fail cryptically. A token that has traded up from
  its launch price sells normally.
</Note>

What a sell delivers is also net of the token's **sell tax**, set
independently of its buy tax - the token page shows both rates. See
[Fees](/fees).

Integrating rather than trading? The exact revert an oversized sell
raises, and how to surface it, is in the
[revert table](/integration/onchain#decoding-reverts).

## Further reading

<Accordion title="Why the floor holds, in one inequality">

The pool opens as a one-sided range above the launch price, so there is no
resting bid put there by the launcher. What supports the price is the
liquidity that arrives as people buy.

For a concentrated position the amount released by a move from
$\sqrt{P_a}$ to $\sqrt{P_b}$ is:

$$
\Delta y = L\,\big(\sqrt{P_a} - \sqrt{P_b}\big)
$$

which is what "depth" means everywhere in this protocol: not the pool's
balance, but **how much comes out for a given move**. A pool holding a
large balance in a narrow band far from the current price is shallow where
it counts.

It is also why the protocol ranks routes by what a move actually releases
rather than by pool size - the two disagree often, and only one of them
is what a trader experiences.

</Accordion>
