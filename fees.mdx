Every trade on a BaseStonk pool pays the fee its creator set at launch.
Transfers between wallets are free. The fee is built into the pool
itself, so there is no route around it.

<Warning>
**The fee is a percentage of each trade's volume - not a share of a
pool's trading fee.** On a \$10,000 swap against a token routing 3% to
holders, \$300 flows to holders - not 3% of some small LP fee. This is
what makes the dividend numbers on the
[baskets page](/dividend-baskets) as large as they are.
</Warning>

## Simple launches

| | Rate |
|---|---|
| Trade fee | **1%** |
| → creator's half - by default paid to holders, half of that via the \$BSTONK basket | 0.5% |
| → to the platform | 0.5% |

By default the creator's half goes to holder dividends, with half of that
routed through the \$BSTONK basket. The creator can redirect it to a fee
address instead, and that address can be handed to a new owner later.

<Note>
**On Robinhood Chain there is no \$BSTONK basket**, so the creator's half
is holder dividends or a fee address and nothing else. The platform's
half is converted to USDG and held on that chain rather than burnt as
\$BSTONK. The rates are identical; only the destinations differ.
[Chains &rarr;](/chains#where-the-platforms-cut-goes)
</Note>

## Advanced launches

The creator sets two rates - a **buy tax** and a **sell tax**, each
anything up to **10%**, independent of each other - and divides the
creator's share between payees, buyback & burn, liquidity, and holder
dividends. The split is one recipe applied to whichever rate a trade
paid; only the rate differs by direction.

The platform's cut of each trade is **min(half that trade's rate, 1%)**:

| Rate the trade paid | Platform takes | Creator controls |
|---|---|---|
| 1% | 0.5% | 0.5% |
| 4% | 1% | 3% |
| 10% | 1% | 9% |

A token taxing 1% on buys and 10% on sells pays the platform 0.5% of each
buy and 1% of each sell.

<Note>
  Both rates and the split are enforced by the fee hook inside the swap
  itself. Nothing about them can be changed after launch - by anyone.
</Note>

## The anti-sniper toll

A launch can arm a decaying toll on its opening minutes: buys inside the
window pay a rate falling from up to 99% down to the buy tax. The
platform's cut stays capped at 1% throughout - everything above it flows
through the creator's own split. Sells never pay the toll.
See [Launching](/launching#the-anti-sniper-toll).

## Where the fee is taken

The hook takes the fee during the swap, inside the pool - so it cannot be
dodged by trading through another route, and a bot that quotes without it
reverts. Integrators: see
[the three things every integration must handle](/integration/onchain#three-things-every-integration-must-handle).

## Further reading

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="The platform cut, as a formula">

The platform's share is capped, so a creator who raises their tax keeps
every basis point above the cap:

$$
\text{platform} = \min\!\left(\frac{\tau}{2},\ 100\ \text{bps}\right)
\qquad
\text{creator} = \tau - \text{platform}
$$

where $\tau$ is the rate the trade paid in basis points -
$\tau_{\text{buy}}$ or $\tau_{\text{sell}}$, each $0 \le \tau \le 1000$.

The consequence is that the platform's take as a *proportion* of the tax
falls away as the tax rises - 50% of a 1% tax, 25% of a 4% tax, 10% of a
10% tax. There is no tier, no negotiation and no discount; it is the same
arithmetic for every launch.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why volume, not market cap, sets what holders earn">

A holder's dividend flow is a function of **turnover**, not price:

$$
\text{paid to holders per day} = V_{24h} \times \frac{r}{10{,}000}
$$

where $V_{24h}$ is 24-hour volume in dollars and $r$ is the rewards share
in basis points.

Nothing in that expression contains market cap. A $2M token turning over
its own cap once a day pays holders more than a $50M token that barely
trades. This is why the figures on the baskets page look large next to a
conventional "LP fee share" - those pay a fraction of a fraction of
volume, and this pays a fraction of volume.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What a trader actually receives">

For a swap of size $x$ against a pool taxing $\tau$ in that direction:

$$
\text{out} = \big(1 - \tfrac{\tau}{10{,}000}\big) \cdot f_{\text{pool}}(x)
$$

where $f_{\text{pool}}$ is the ordinary constant-product or concentrated
curve, and $\tau$ is the buy or sell rate as the direction demands. The
tax is applied by the hook **inside** the swap, so it composes with price
impact rather than replacing it.

An aggregator that quotes $f_{\text{pool}}(x)$ alone and sets `minOut`
from it will revert every time - the quote is right about the curve and
wrong about the pool.

</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>
