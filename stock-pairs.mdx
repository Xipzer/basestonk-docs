Pair a launch with a tokenized equity and your token is quoted in shares
of it. The chart moves with the stock, and the opening price is read from
a live on-chain feed at the moment you sign.

pick it.

## How a pair becomes launchable

Every launchable pair carries an on-chain price source - a Chainlink feed
where one exists, or a **routed price feed** where one does not: a small
on-chain contract that walks a fixed route through real pools and derives
the price from what those pools would actually pay.

Either way, a feed whose answer is stale or dishonest refuses rather than
guessing - and a launch priced off a refusing feed simply does not
happen.

## Routed feeds, and why a pair can go quiet

A routed feed does not just read a price - it measures **depth**, and it
refuses to answer when the market behind it is too thin to be honest.

Each hop of the route is measured by how much can actually be sold into
its pool before the price moves materially. Every feed is deployed with a
floor, and if any hop falls under it the feed reverts with `HopTooThin`
rather than returning a number.

<Note>
This is the design working, not a fault. The alternative is a launch
priced off a pool that a \$500 trade could move - which is how a token
opens at a market cap that was never real. A feed that refuses is a feed
telling the truth about its own market.
</Note>

When it happens, the create form shows a **"Too thin"** badge on the
pair and blocks the launch button, with the reason stated.

It is not permanent and nothing is stuck. **The pair reopens by itself
the moment its market deepens** - no action from the creator or from
BaseStonk. Tokens already launched against that pair are completely
unaffected: the feed only prices the *opening* of a new pool, never one
that exists.

A pair whose market has thinned for good is re-curated onto a deeper
venue rather than having its floor lowered - lowering the floor would
keep the pair open by agreeing to price it badly.

## Stocks as dividend assets

Pairs are not the only place equities appear. Stocks and RWAs can also be
**dividend assets**: a launch can pay its holders in tokenized equities
through its payout basket. See [Wedges](/wedges).

<Note>
  \$BSTONK itself is priced by a purpose-built adapter reading the
  BSTONK/USDC pool's own price times the USDC feed. It is a spot price: a
  determined trader can move it within a transaction, which can misprice
  the *opening* of a new launch that chose this pair - never a pool that
  already exists.
</Note>

## Further reading

<Accordion title="How a stock-paired chart is priced">

A stock-paired launch reads its opening price through a Chainlink feed, so
the same market cap in dollars implies a different token price depending on
what the share is worth that day:

$$
p_0 = \frac{M}{S \cdot P_{\text{share}}}
$$

Equity feeds carry a **96-hour** staleness bound rather than the usual 24,
because equity markets close over the weekend. A 24-hour bound would
fail closed every Saturday and refuse every launch until Monday.

If a feed cannot answer at all, the launch reverts. That is deliberate: a
launch priced off a dead oracle is worse than a launch that did not happen.

</Accordion>
