\$BSTONK is BaseStonk's own token, launched through the same machinery as
everything else on the venue. Address:
[`0x0F61Edbfe6Cd86024C0f210c0695B08df55fdfc9`](https://basescan.org/address/0x0F61Edbfe6Cd86024C0f210c0695B08df55fdfc9#code)

What makes it different from every other platform token is that **it is not
paid by its own trading alone.** Two mechanisms feed it, and both of them
scale with the whole venue rather than with \$BSTONK's own chart.

<Note>
**Over 9.6% of the total supply has already been burnt** - more than 96
million tokens, permanently, and the number only moves one way. The live
figure is on the \$BSTONK token page under *Platform flywheel*; the one
quoted here is a checkpoint, and it is always an underestimate.
</Note>

## The dual flywheel

Most tokens have a buyback. \$BSTONK has two, running independently.

<Columns cols={2}>

<Card title="Flywheel one: its own tax" icon="arrows-rotate">
A quarter of every \$BSTONK trade is spent buying \$BSTONK and burning it -
**inside the swap**, by the hook, in the same transaction. No treasury, no
multisig, nobody deciding when.

Scales with: \$BSTONK's own volume.
</Card>

<Card title="Flywheel two: the whole platform" icon="globe">
The platform's cut of **every launch on the venue** - in whatever token that
launch pays in - is sold, converted to \$BSTONK, and burnt. Automatically,
on a schedule, without anyone pressing anything.

Scales with: every token that ever launches here.
</Card>

</Columns>

The second one is the part no competitor has. A launchpad normally takes its
fee and keeps it. Here, **the platform's revenue is a standing bid on its own
token, and everything it buys is destroyed.** A launch that has nothing to do
with \$BSTONK still burns \$BSTONK, simply by trading.

That is why the burn total is where it is. It is not one token's volume - it
is the venue's.

## The dual dividends

The same doubling applies to what holders are paid.

<Columns cols={2}>

<Card title="Stream one: USDC, automatically" icon="dollar-sign">
A quarter of every \$BSTONK trade is paid to holders as **USDC** - not as
more of the token, not as a claim on something. Real dollars, delivered by
the contract.
</Card>

<Card title="Stream two: the platform basket" icon="basket-shopping">
\$BSTONK holders also receive from the **basket vault** - a portfolio built
from a share of participating launches' rewards across the entire platform.
</Card>

</Columns>

<Note>
The basket vault currently carries **62 asset slots, 57 of them holding a
balance**. It is the largest single basket on the platform, and it grows
two ways at once: **wider** as new launches route a share into it, and
**deeper** as those launches trade.
</Note>

Every other basket on BaseStonk belongs to one token and is fed by one
token's volume. This one is fed by all of them. A holder is not betting on a
single launch doing well - they hold a claim on the venue's aggregate
activity, paid out in kind.

## Supply only moves down

Supply was fixed at launch. **There is no mint function and no owner.**
Between the two flywheels and voluntary burns from the fee wallet, the only
direction the number moves is down - and it has already moved more than
nine and a half percent.

## A pair, not just a token

\$BSTONK is a registered launch pair: new tokens can be priced in it, pay
their fees in it, and pay their holder dividends in it. Launches paired in
\$BSTONK can still pay dividends in any other admitted asset through the same
conversion engine that serves every basket.

## Further reading

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="The two burn rates, as formulae">

The in-contract wedge burns as a function of \$BSTONK's own turnover:

$$
\text{burn}_{\text{self}} = \frac{V_{\text{BSTONK}} \cdot w_{\text{burn}}}{p}
$$

with $w_{\text{burn}} = 25\ \text{bps}$ and $p$ the price at the moment of
the swap.

The platform sweep burns as a function of **every other token's** turnover:

$$
\text{burn}_{\text{platform}} = \frac{1}{p}\sum_{i} V_i \cdot \min\!\left(\frac{\tau_i}{2},\,100\ \text{bps}\right)
$$

summed over every launch $i$ on the venue, where $\tau_i$ is that launch's
tax. The two are additive and independent:

$$
\text{burn}_{\text{total}} = \text{burn}_{\text{self}} + \text{burn}_{\text{platform}}
$$

The second term has no ceiling that depends on \$BSTONK. It grows with the
number of launches and their volume, which is why the burn curve steepens as
the venue does rather than tracking one chart.

Note the $1/p$ in both: **the same dollar burns more tokens at a lower
price.** Buy pressure is strongest exactly when the price is weakest.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why a platform-wide basket is a different asset class">

A single-token basket pays a holder in proportion to one token's volume:

$$
\text{flow}_{\text{single}} = V_{\text{token}} \cdot \frac{r}{10{,}000}
$$

The platform basket sums over the venue:

$$
\text{flow}_{\text{basket}} = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{L}} V_i \cdot \frac{r_i}{10{,}000} \cdot \nu_i
$$

where $\mathcal{L}$ is the set of participating launches and $\nu_i$ is the
share each routes to the vault.

The distinction is not size, it is **variance**. A single launch's flow is
one random variable; the basket's is a sum over many, so idiosyncratic
failures wash out and what remains is the venue's aggregate activity. Adding
a launch adds a term - it never removes one.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How a vault round is allocated">

A round pays each eligible holder:

$$
\text{payout}_h = A \cdot \frac{w_h}{\sum_j w_j}
\qquad
w_h = \sum_{t \in [t_0,\,t_1]} b_h(t)\,\Delta t
$$

so weight is balance **integrated over time**, not a snapshot. A snapshot is
trivially sniped - buy before it, sell after - and the block is public when
the round commits.

Rounds are also paid in tranches on a rolling schedule rather than
emptying the vault at once, so the vault always carries a balance and no
single round is worth timing.

</Accordion>
