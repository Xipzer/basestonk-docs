A BaseStonk launch can pay its holders in **up to ten assets of the
creator's choosing** - NVIDIA, gold ETFs, cbBTC, \$BSTONK, agent tokens,
any mix at once. Most launchpads pay rewards in whatever the token trades
against, or not at all.

## How it works

1. **The creator picks the basket at launch** - up to ten assets with
   weights, from a menu of several hundred.
2. **A share of every trade flows into the basket.**
3. **The contracts buy the assets on-chain** - through real markets,
   with a price floor so holders can never be quietly shortchanged. No
   bot, no middleman, no fee on conversions.
4. **Everyone gets their share automatically.** There is nothing to
   claim - payouts arrive as people trade.

## The arithmetic - volume, not fee-share

The single most misread fact about BaseStonk dividends: **they are a
percentage of trading volume, not a cut of a pool's LP fee.**

A token routing 3% to holders sends **\$300** of a \$10,000 buy straight
to its holders, on that one trade. Volume compounds daily; market cap
does not have to.

What that adds up to at a 3% holder share:

| Daily volume | Annual volume | Stock delivered to holders / year | At \$2M cap | At \$5M cap | At \$10M cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| \$200k | \$73M | ~\$2.2M | 110% | 44% | 22% |
| \$500k | \$182M | ~\$5.5M | 275% | 110% | 55% |
| \$1M | \$365M | ~\$11M | 550% | 220% | 110% |

That \$2.2M is not more of the meme token - it is **real tokenized stock**,
converted on-chain and pushed to holders' wallets as people trade. That is
the entire pitch.

<Note>
This is arithmetic, not a promise. Volume is the input the model cannot
guarantee: a token that stops trading stops paying. What the contracts do
guarantee is the percentage - the hook takes it inside the swap, the split
is immutable after launch, and the conversion to basket assets is
floor-guarded on-chain.
</Note>

## The controller seat

The basket's **composition** sits behind a controller seat. The creator
holds it by default and can re-weight or swap the basket's assets on a
live token, hand the seat to someone else, or renounce it.

What the seat can never touch is the **percentage of volume** flowing to
holders: that is fixed at launch and enforced by the hook, seat or no
seat.

Two one-way doors close the seat:

- **Renounce at launch** - tick the box and the basket the launch
  declared is the basket forever.
- **Renounce later** - the same one-way door, any time. There is no
  undo, for anyone - including the platform.

Whether the seat is live or renounced is public on-chain state, so anyone
can check whether a token's payout mix is a policy or a constant.

Two things worth knowing before rebuilding a basket:

- **Nothing in flight is lost.** Value sitting in a replaced slot still
  reaches holders on the same sharing maths. A rebuild changes where
  future dividends go, never where past ones went.
- **A careless pick degrades gracefully.** A slot whose asset cannot be
  priced simply pays the pair currency instead - nothing gets trapped.

Driving the seat from code - the calls, what they enforce, and the
readable state - is on the
[on-chain page](/integration/onchain#the-basket-controller-seat).

## The honest edges

- The basket is the creator's **preference, not a guarantee of which
  asset arrives**. If an asset can't be bought at a fair price right now,
  that share is paid in the pair currency instead - visibly, never lost.
- A conversion that would land below fair value is refused and retried
  smaller, rather than accepted badly.
- An empty basket just means classic behaviour - dividends in the pair.
- If the controller seat is still live, the payout mix can change. Anyone
  can check on-chain whether the seat has been renounced.

## Where to see it

Any token whose card carries the **BASKET REWARDS** tag pays a basket. Its token page shows the designated assets and weights, each holder's running totals per asset, and a breakdown of what has been received and what is waiting.

<Note>
The platform's own token participates too: launches can route a share of their rewards into the \$BSTONK basket vault - a portfolio paid out to \$BSTONK holders as the assets themselves.
</Note>

<Accordion title="How a holder's share is computed">

Rewards accrue against an accumulator rather than being pushed to every
wallet, because pushing to thousands of holders on every trade would cost
more gas than it distributes.

The contract tracks a running total per share:

$$
\text{acc} \mathrel{+}= \frac{\Delta_{\text{in}}}{S_{\text{total}}}
$$

and a holder's claimable balance is the distance the accumulator has moved
since they were last settled:

$$
\text{owed}(h) = s_h \cdot \big(\text{acc} - \text{acc}_h\big)
$$

where $s_h$ is the holder's shares and $\text{acc}_h$ is their checkpoint.
Both update whenever they trade or claim, so the arithmetic is $O(1)$ per
holder no matter how many holders exist.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What the conversion is allowed to accept">

Every automatic conversion has a floor, and the floor - not a slippage
setting - is what bounds it:

$$
P = \min\!\big(P_{\text{gate}},\ P_{\text{market}}\big)
$$

The floor is that price less the route's real venue fees and a small
slippage allowance that scales with the route and is capped overall.

Taking the *lower* of the two prices matters more than it looks. Checking
that two prices agree only requires an attacker to move both; taking the
minimum requires them to move the one that is already lowest, which is the
expensive direction.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why a refusal is the design working">

A conversion whose size cannot clear the floor is refused, and the
contract retries at smaller sizes across later turns. So a slot with a
thin market converts in small pieces rather than accepting a bad price
once.

If a route cannot clear the floor at any size, the slot **pays in the pair
token instead**. Value always reaches holders; only the currency changes.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Time-weighted allocation, for basket vault rounds">

Vault rounds weight a holder by balance *and* duration, not by a snapshot:

$$
w_h = \sum_{t \in [t_0,\,t_1]} b_h(t)\,\Delta t
\qquad
\text{share}_h = \frac{w_h}{\sum_j w_j}
$$

A single-block snapshot is trivially sniped - buy immediately before it,
sell immediately after - and the block is announced on chain when the round
commits. Integrating over the window removes the edge entirely: to earn a
full share you have to actually hold for the window.

</Accordion>
