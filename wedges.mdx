A launch splits its trade tax into *wedges*. Each wedge runs inside the
trading machinery itself - none of them is a bot that can be switched off.
Every wedge on this page exists on both Base and Robinhood Chain, except
one, marked below.

<Note>
**Wedges are not Advanced-only.** Advanced mode is what lets you set the
sizes of every wedge by hand. A Simple launch gets the dividend and
\$BSTONK-basket wedges switched on by default, and the max wallet cap, on
its fixed 1% fee. See [Simple vs Advanced](/launching#simple-vs-advanced).
</Note>

## Holder dividends

Part of every trade's tax goes to a contract that pays **every holder
their share, in the assets the creator picked** - up to 10 of them:
tokenized stocks, majors, ecosystem tokens. If a basket is empty or an
asset can't be priced, holders get the pair currency instead. Never more
of the token itself.

- Payouts arrive automatically as people trade. There is nothing to
  claim.
- Anyone who wants their balance immediately can claim it themselves -
  optional, never required.
- Your own running total shows on the token's page when your wallet is
  connected.

## \$BSTONK basket wedge (Base only)

On Base, a launch can route a share of its holder rewards into the
platform basket vault. Like every other wedge, the share is fixed at launch
and enforced by the hook. It does not exist on Robinhood Chain: \$BSTONK
and its vault are Base contracts, and the platform's cut there is held as
USDG instead. [Chains &rarr;](/chains#where-the-platforms-cut-goes)

## Buyback & burn

A burn wedge spends its share of every trade buying the token back from
its own pool and sending it to the burn address - **in the same
transaction as the trade that funded it**.

Every burn is a public on-chain event. The token page shows the cumulative
amount burned and bought back under *Tokenomics*.

## Liquidity

A liquidity wedge deepens the token's own pool with its share of the tax,
raising the floor under later sellers.

## Max wallet

A launch can cap how much any one wallet may hold:

- **Buys** that would breach the cap revert.
- **Sells are never blocked** by it, whatever a holder owns.
- The creator and the pool itself are exempt so the mechanics keep
  working.

<Note>
  B20-mode launches ship without max wallet and without holder dividends -
  the chain-native standard has no room for them. Tax, buyback & burn and
  fee splits work unchanged. See [B20 mode](/b20-mode).
</Note>

## Further reading

<Accordion title="How the wedges divide a tax">

The tax splits into named wedges that must sum to the whole:

$$
\tau = w_{\text{payees}} + w_{\text{burn}} + w_{\text{liquidity}} + w_{\text{rewards}}
$$

Each is set at launch and **immutable afterwards**. The hook reads them
inside the swap, so no route around the pool can pay a different split.

A buyback wedge spends its share buying the token and sending it to the
dead address, so the effect on supply is:

$$
S_{t+1} = S_t - \frac{V \cdot w_{\text{burn}}}{10{,}000 \cdot p_t}
$$

Burn pressure therefore scales with **volume and inversely with price** -
the same dollar burns more tokens when the price is lower, which is when it
matters most.

</Accordion>
