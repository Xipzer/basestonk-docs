A launch can route the creator's dev buy into a **vesting vault** instead
of their wallet: a per-launch contract that releases the tokens linearly,
with an optional cliff before anything unlocks at all. Pick 7, 30, 90 or
365 days from the presets, or type any duration - **7 days is the floor
and there is no ceiling.**

Dev-buy vesting is identical on Base and Robinhood Chain - the same vault contract, the same schedule rules, the same `VaultCreated` event.

<Note>
**Vesting is on the Simple form too.** Both modes carry the same vest
fields and the chain honours them identically.
</Note>

The point is not the lock - it is the **disclosure**. The schedule is
announced on-chain in the launch transaction itself, so it is public from
the block the token exists: a buyer reading the token page sees exactly
what the creator locked and until when, before deciding anything.

## The honest trade

<Warning>
  A vested dev buy is **uncapped by design**. The 10% dev-buy cap applies
  only to the unvested path - tokens paid straight to the creator's
  wallet. Choose a lock and the whole buy, whatever its size, routes into
  the vault. The cap protected buyers from an undisclosed large position;
  the vault replaces that protection with a disclosed one: the size and
  the schedule are on-chain in the launch transaction, and the schedule
  can never be shortened.
</Warning>

A vested launch is not automatically safer than a capped one - it is
differently safe. The creator may hold more than 10%, but every token of
it is time-locked on a schedule the chain announced at birth, and the
launcher refuses a vesting request without a dev buy to vest
(`VestRequiresDevBuy`).

## How the vault behaves

One vault per launch, created by the launcher. The contract enforces:

- **Linear release.** Nothing before the cliff; after it, tokens vest
  continuously, and anyone can trigger the release of what has vested.
- **Extend, never shorten.** The beneficiary can lengthen a schedule;
  any change that would shorten one reverts.
- **Add, never subtract.** The beneficiary can lock more tokens under
  new schedules. Each addition is its own on-chain event.
- **The seat can move.** The vault can be transferred to another
  address - a project outliving its founding wallet.

## Dividends while locked

Locked tokens are still held tokens, so a vault on a dividend-paying
launch **earns holder rewards** like any other holder. The beneficiary
can claim what the vault has earned, per asset - the locked tokens
themselves never move early.

Two ways that stream ends, both one-way:

- **Renouncing it** - the beneficiary gives the locked supply's dividend
  claim to the other holders, forever. It cannot be restored, not even by
  the platform.
- **Takeover.** If the community
  [takes over the token](/cto), a departed founder's locked supply stops
  earning the moment the seat moves. The tokens themselves remain the
  beneficiary's and keep vesting on their schedule; only the dividend
  stream ends.

## Reading a vault

Everything above is public state on the vault contract: every schedule's
total, released amount, start, cliff and duration; what a release would
send right now; whether the dividend stream is gone. The factory's
`vaultOf(token)` answers whether a token has a vault at all -
`address(0)` means the dev buy was not vested.
