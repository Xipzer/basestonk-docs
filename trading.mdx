Trading on BaseStonk is wallet-to-pool through a router that custodies
nothing. There is no deposit and no account.

## Pay with whatever you hold

On crypto-paired tokens the trade panel accepts **ETH, WETH or USDC**
directly: it routes your asset into the token's pair through an
aggregator, then completes the pool swap - one flow, one confirmation.

Most stock-paired tokens route from ETH the same way. The exception is
the handful of Coinbase B20 stocks with no on-chain market yet - for
those you must hold the stock already, and the panel says so rather than
failing. See [stock pairs](/stock-pairs).

## Charts and data

Every token page carries live candles, trades, holders and the token's
tax mechanics. The figures come from BaseStonk's own indexer reading the
chain - the same numbers the [public API](/integration/api) serves.

## Fees you pay when trading

The token's launch-time tax (see [Fees](/fees)) is taken inside the swap -
buy and sell rates independently, on V6 launches. The interface shows the
token's rates on its page - there are no other platform charges on top.

## Further reading

<Accordion title="What price impact costs you, and why size matters more than slippage settings">

For a constant-product pool, buying $x$ of the pair moves you along the
curve and the output is:

$$
\Delta_{\text{out}} = \frac{y \cdot x}{X + x}
$$

so your effective price worsens with $x$ **relative to the pool**, not in
absolute dollars. A $500 trade is small in a deep pool and enormous in a
thin one.

The practical rule: a 2% move is the useful yardstick. If your trade size
is near what it takes to move the pool 2%, expect to feel it - and split
the order rather than raising slippage tolerance, which only widens what
you will accept, not what you will pay.

</Accordion>
