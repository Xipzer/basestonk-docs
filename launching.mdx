Every launch mints a fixed supply of **1,000,000,000 tokens** and opens a
Uniswap v4 pool seeded with 99% of it. The pair you pick is what your
chart is quoted in forever.

## What happens when you sign

One transaction does all of it:

1. **Your token is created** and its pool opens at the market cap you
   chose, converted to a pool price using the pair's live on-chain price.
2. **Your dev buy executes**, if you asked for one - nobody can get in
   ahead of you.
3. **The launcher proves it holds nothing.** The transaction reverts if a
   single token is left in it - which is why the liquidity can never be
   pulled.

<Warning>
  Opening market caps run \$500 to \$10,000 for standard pairs and \$250
  to \$15,000 for gated ecosystem pairs. On some stock pairs, very low
  caps are refused - the form warns you and shows the exact opening
  price before you sign.
</Warning>

## Simple vs Advanced

The two modes differ in **one thing: who sets the tax rate and how its
proceeds are divided.** Everything else on the form is available in both.

**Simple** fixes the trade fee at 1% - 0.5% to you, 0.5% to the platform.
Your half is paid to your holders as dividends by default; a single fee
address field redirects it to a wallet instead.

**Advanced** sets its own rates: separate buy and sell sliders, each up
to 10%. Your cut divides between:

- up to **8 payee wallets** with custom shares
- a **buyback & burn** wedge
- a **liquidity** wedge
- **holder dividends**, paid through a payout basket of up to 10 assets
- a **\$BSTONK basket** wedge - a share routed into the platform vault

The rates and the split are fixed at launch and enforced by the pool's
hook - it is not a promise, it is the contract. Details in [Fees](/fees)
and [Wedges](/wedges).

### What both modes have

Everything below is on the form in **Simple as well as Advanced**:

- the **dev buy**, and **[vesting](/vesting)** to lock it
- the **anti-sniper toll**
- **holder dividends**, the **payout basket**, and the **\$BSTONK
  basket** share - Simple simply starts with them switched on
- **renounce basket control at launch**
- the **max wallet** cap
- **B20 mode**, when the pair is a B20

A Simple launch that changes nothing still ships dividends, a basket, and
a max wallet.

## Your own buy

You can buy your own token in the launch transaction itself, so nobody
front-runs the opening. Two things catch people out:

<Warning>
**The amount is in the pair token, not dollars.** If your token is priced
in AAPLc, typing `123` asks for 123 AAPLc - not \$123. Read the symbol
beside the box and the *≈ % of supply* badge before signing.
</Warning>

- **The cap is 10% of supply.** The **Max** button fills to 9.9%
  deliberately - the pair price can move between quoting and signing, and
  a buy that lands over the cap reverts the whole launch.
- **The buy pays no trade tax** and no anti-sniper toll.

If you do not hold enough of the pair token, the form offers to swap into
it in the same flow - and for WETH pairs it simply wraps your ETH.

## Lock your dev buy

The dev buy can go into a **vesting vault** instead of your wallet: 7,
30, 90 or 365 days from the presets, or any duration from 7 days up, with
an optional cliff. The schedule is published on-chain in the launch
transaction itself, so every buyer can read exactly what is locked and
until when. It can be extended later, never shortened.

The honest trade: a vested dev buy is **uncapped** - the 10% cap applies
only to the unvested path. The disclosure is the schedule, published at
birth. Full mechanics in [Vesting](/vesting).

## The anti-sniper toll

A launch can arm a decaying toll on its first minutes: pick a window up
to 30 minutes, and buys inside it pay a rate starting at 99% and falling
to your buy tax by the window's end. Sells never pay the toll.

The token page shows a live meter while the toll runs - the current rate
and the countdown - so a human buying early sees exactly what they would
pay. The meter is the difference between a protection and a trap.

## Freeze the basket at launch

The payout basket normally comes with a **controller seat**: the creator
can re-weight or swap its assets later (never the percentage of volume -
that is immutable). Ticking **renounce basket control at launch** burns
that seat in the launch transaction, so the basket the launch declared is
the basket forever. Details in [Dividend baskets](/dividend-baskets).

## After launch

- The **fee address can be handed over** - a token outlives the wallet
  that launched it.
- **Artwork and description** can be updated by whoever holds the
  controller seat - the creator at first, the community after a
  [takeover](/cto).
- The **basket's composition** can be re-tuned by its controller, unless
  the seat was renounced.
- Nothing else can change. The tax rates, the split, the supply and the
  pool are immutable, and there is no admin over a live pool.

<Accordion title="How the opening price is derived from your market cap">

You give a market cap; the pool needs a price. With a fixed supply $S$ and
a pair token worth $P_{\text{pair}}$ dollars:

$$
p_0 = \frac{M}{S \cdot P_{\text{pair}}}
$$

which Uniswap v4 stores as a square root in Q64.96 fixed point:

$$
\sqrt{P}_{X96} = \sqrt{p_0} \cdot 2^{96}
$$

The launcher then opens a one-sided position from $p_0$ upward, so the
first buyer is the first person to move the price - there is no pre-filled
bid to sell into.

The price the conversion uses is read **at execution time**, on-chain:
from the pair's Chainlink feed, from a routed price feed walking real
pools, or from a route the RoutedQuoteGate verified. There is no
client-side price maths to be wrong - and a feed that cannot answer
honestly [refuses, rather than guessing](/stock-pairs#routed-feeds-and-why-a-pair-can-go-quiet).

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why some high-priced pairs have a higher effective minimum">

The launcher materialises an integer count of the pair's smallest unit. For
a pair with $d$ decimals:

$$
\text{raw} = \left\lfloor \frac{M}{10^{\,d}\cdot P_{\text{pair}}} \right\rfloor
$$

and when that floor lands on a small integer, the realised cap steps rather
than moving smoothly. Against an expensive 8-decimal share the practical
floor is roughly **ten times the share price**, which is why the form
refuses some combinations that look valid arithmetically.

The clamp is not cosmetic: it is what stops a launch opening at a price the
pool cannot actually represent.

</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The market-cap band, and why it exists">

The create form offers:

$$
250 \le M \le 15{,}000 \quad \text{(gated pairs)}
\qquad
500 \le M \le 10{,}000 \quad \text{(standard)}
$$

while the V6 contract enforces $100 \le M \le 250{,}000$ on standard
pairs and the gated band exactly as the form shows it - the form's
tighter band is the range where the number stays honest.

The upper bound is the interesting one. A creator who could name any
opening cap would name an enormous one - it costs nothing and puts their
token at the top of every market-cap sort. The band makes the number
mean something.

</Accordion>
